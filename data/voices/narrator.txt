You're born in Osaka, Japan. Your father was Chinese, a factory worker who disappeared when you were four. Left for a pack of cigarettes and never came back. Your mother is Japanese, works double shifts at a laundry service, comes home smelling like detergent and exhaustion.

In Japan, blood matters. Yours is wrong—half-Chinese, impure. The kids at school have a word for people like you. They use it often. Your teachers hear everything. They do nothing. You learn early that some people are born belonging. You were born outside, looking in through glass you can't break.

The beating happens when you're 15. Three older kids from a different school corner you in a park near the train station. They've seen you before. They don't like your face. They take turns: fists, kicks, a wooden stick one of them brought from home. You curl up on the ground and wait. That's when you notice the man. Black suit...
